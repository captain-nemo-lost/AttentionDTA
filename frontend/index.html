📌 Methodological Caveats & Verification:
• 2-of-5-Folds Caveat: Reproduced Davis S1 random-split metrics reflect the average of 2 cross-validation folds (Fold 1: 0.6682, Fold 2: 0.7024) out of 5 standard folds due to local hardware compute limits, whereas Zhao et al. (2023) evaluated across all 5 complete folds.
• Single-Seed Caveat: Drug-disjoint cold-start evaluation (R² = -0.0079) was conducted with a single random seed on 14 held-out novel drugs (higher variance than multi-seed cross-validation).
• Sequence Verification: Target kinase sequences in benchmark data are verified against canonical UniProt entries (BRAF: P15056, FLT3: P36888).
• Model Execution: Predictions and cross-attention heatmaps are generated by an authentic PyTorch AttentionDTA model with sequence padding strictly masked.

🔑 Key Scientific Takeaways

  • S1 Reproduction Confirmed: Our reproduced model achieves an average R² ≈ 0.6853 on Davis, directionally consistent with the published 0.7409.
  • The Cold-Start Collapse: When tested on 14 completely unseen drugs (drug-disjoint split), R² collapses to -0.0079.
  • Why This Matters: Deep attention models often overfit to chemical scaffold identities in the training set rather than learning universal physical binding dynamics.

📢 The 3-Minute Elevator Pitch

  1. The Problem: Testing cancer inhibitor drugs in wet labs takes years and billions of dollars. We use machine learning to predict drug binding strength directly from sequence text.
  2. The Architecture: AttentionDTA embeds SMILES (100 tokens) and amino acids (1,200 tokens), extracts local motifs with 1D-CNNs, matches them with Two-Sided Multi-Head Cross-Attention, and predicts affinity (pK_d) via a 5-layer MLP.
  3. Group 17 Finding: While the model performs well on known chemical families (R² ≈ 0.69), it collapses on 14 novel unseen drugs (R² ≈ -0.01). This proves sequence models tend to memorize chemical templates rather than true binding physics.

❓ Top 5 Viva Questions & Spoken Answers

Q1: What is the difference between DTI and DTA?
Answer: DTI is binary classification ('Does it bind? Yes/No'). DTA is continuous regression predicting exact physical binding strength (pK_d = -log10(K_d)). DTA is clinically much more useful because it tells you drug potency in nanomolar concentrations.
Q2: What is the Attention mechanism doing here?
Answer: It computes an interaction matrix between every atom of the drug and every residue of the protein: A = tanh(W_d * D * (W_p * P)^T). The row-sum shows active drug atoms, and the column-sum shows the protein binding pocket.
Q3: What does R² ≤ 0 mean in the Cold-Start experiment?
Answer: R² measures variance explained compared to a naive mean predictor. R² = 1 is perfect; R² = 0 means the model performs no better than just guessing the dataset average (pKd ≈ 5.4). R² = -0.01 means complete failure to generalize to unseen chemical structures.
Q4: What loss function and optimizer did you use?
Answer: Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss, with Adam optimizer (learning rate 0.001) trained on the 24,956 drug-protein pairs of the Davis kinase dataset.
Q5: What are the convolutional layers doing before attention?
Answer: Three 1D-CNN layers with 32, 64, and 96 filters extract local n-grams (functional groups in the drug, and secondary structure alpha-helix/beta-sheet motifs in the protein).
